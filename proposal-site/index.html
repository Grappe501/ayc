Arkansas Youth Coalition
× Arkansas Civic Action Network

A proposal to connect ENGAGE high school civic learning with sustained, nonpartisan voter participation and leadership opportunities for Arkansas young adults ages 18–24.

Presented for consideration by Dr. Gail Choate and the ArkCAN Board · Proposed by Kelly Grappe

01 · Partnership rationale

One continuum of civic learning and participation

ArkCAN's ENGAGE program provides five three-hour civic academy sessions and student-directed service learning. AYC's existing youth leadership architecture can provide a distinct, voluntary next step for young adults after high school: nonpartisan voter registration assistance, election information, civic volunteering and leadership practice.

ENGAGE · High school

Civic knowledge, media literacy, advocacy skills, service projects and mentorship.

AYC · Ages 18–24

Registration assistance, voting-process education, neutral election reminders and community-based leadership.

Shared long-term outcome

Young Arkansans with practical civic skills and enduring opportunities for participation.

02 · Proposed program

Nonpartisan young-adult voter participation

AYC would concentrate on accessible, accurate voter registration and election-process education for eligible young adults ages 18–24. Activities would include publicly available registration guidance, assistance checking registration, neutral voting-date and polling-location information, nonpartisan community workshops, and voluntary civic leadership opportunities.

AYC would use ArkCAN's GET VOTE READY resources where authorized. All outreach would be politically neutral and available without regard to party, candidate preference or viewpoint. No candidate endorsement, partisan voter targeting, or transfer of ENGAGE minors' records into voter outreach.

Proposed first-year operating sequence

First 60 days

Board authorization, legal and fiscal agreement, youth advisory design, privacy review, baseline metrics, pilot site selection.

Months 3–6

Train youth volunteers, run nonpartisan registration and education pilots, establish reporting and quality assurance.

Months 7–12

Expand with demonstrated capacity, maintain nonpartisan election-information outreach, publish audited program outputs.

03 · Proposed governance

Distinct youth identity, accountable charitable oversight

ArkCAN would serve as the legal and fiscal home for a designated AYC charitable program, subject to its board's approval. AYC would retain a distinct program identity and youth leadership advisory structure. ArkCAN would control charitable expenditures, grants, personnel arrangements, compliance and donor acknowledgments.

Kelly Grappe proposes consideration for appointment to ArkCAN's board, subject to bylaws and independent board approval, with a fundraising and organizational-development portfolio. Board service, compensation if any, and related-party decisions must be handled through conflict-of-interest procedures. A board seat is not a prerequisite for a lawful partnership.

Approval is not presumed. This site is a proposal, not a claim that ArkCAN has accepted AYC or appointed any individual to its board.
04 · Resource development

A diversified funding model

Potential support channels include local philanthropic grants for civic education and leadership, unrestricted individual donations, monthly supporters, employer matching gifts, community sponsorships, and in-kind venue or technology contributions. All foundation guidelines and live application windows require verification before solicitation.
